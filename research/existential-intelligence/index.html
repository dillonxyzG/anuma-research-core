Anuma Research · Existential Intelligence

Existential
Intelligence

A proposed research framework for investigating intelligence through the interaction of context, memory, relationships and agency.

Central Research Question
How does a system develop intelligence when its existence, relationships, history and capacity to act become part of the problem it must navigate?

01 — Framework

Within the Anuma research programme, Existential Intelligence extends the Transactional–Relational framework by bringing context, memory, relationships and agency into a common model.

EI = f(C, M, R, A)

This equation is a proposed operational model. It is intended to provide a structure through which existential intelligence can become a subject of mathematical and empirical investigation.

02 — Four Components

C — Context The surrounding conditions within which a system exists, interprets information and acts.
M — Memory Retained information or history that allows previous states and interactions to influence future behaviour.
R — Relationship The connections through which the system participates in a larger relational structure.
A — Agency The capacity of the system to act, respond, adapt or participate in transformation.

The model therefore moves beyond a representation of intelligence as isolated computation and investigates the conditions under which a system is situated.

03 — Context

Context determines the conditions under which information, relationships and actions acquire significance.

Information + Context → Interpretation

The same signal can therefore produce different system responses when the surrounding state or context changes.

Within the Anuma framework, context is consequently treated as a component of intelligence rather than merely as external metadata.

04 — Memory

Memory introduces temporal continuity.

Past → Memory → Present → Future

Without some mechanism of continuity, each interaction can be treated as an isolated event. Memory permits previous interactions to influence subsequent states.

In the broader Anuma architecture, memory is therefore closely connected to relationship and context.

05 — Relationship

A system does not necessarily exist as an isolated object. It can exist within networks of interaction.

G = (V, E, W)

Here, V represents entities, E represents relationships and W represents relationship weights.

Existential Intelligence therefore inherits an important principle from the Transactional–Relational Intelligence framework: relationships can become part of the structure through which intelligence develops.

06 — Agency

Agency introduces the capacity for a system to participate in its own transformation through action or response.

State → Perception → Decision → Action → New State

The inclusion of agency distinguishes a system that merely receives information from one that can participate in the evolution of its state.

In the proposed Existential Intelligence model, agency is therefore treated as one component of the system's capacity to navigate context and relationships.

07 — Existential State

Combining the four components gives the proposed existential intelligence representation:

EI = f(C, M, R, A)

The function f represents the relationship among these variables. Its specific mathematical form remains an empirical research question.

Research hypothesis

If context, memory, relationships and agency contribute information relevant to future system states, then models incorporating these variables may explain aspects of system behaviour that are not captured by transactional variables alone.

08 — Existential Intelligence as a Dynamic Process

Existential Intelligence is not represented as a fixed quantity. It can be treated as a dynamic function of an evolving system.

EI,t = f(Ct, Mt, Rt, At)

The system then changes through interaction:

𝒮ₜ₊₁ = F(Xₜ, Rₜ, Mₜ, Cₜ, Aₜ, ρₜ)

Consequently, existential intelligence is connected to the broader RTT transformation process.

09 — Meaning and Coordination

The Anuma research architecture proposes a progression:

Information → Intelligence → Meaning → Coordination

Existential Intelligence occupies the part of this research programme concerned with the relationship between system state, context, history, relationships and agency.

The framework does not assume that meaning can be reduced to a single numerical quantity. Instead, it asks whether aspects of meaning-oriented behaviour can be represented through measurable system variables and relationships.

10 — Individual and Collective Intelligence

The framework can be applied to systems at different scales.

Individual System Context, memory, relationships and agency within a single adaptive system.
Networked System Multiple systems interacting through structured relationships.
Living System A system whose state changes through continuous interaction with its environment.
Civilizational System Large-scale networks in which institutions, communities, environments and technologies interact over time.

This scaling question connects Existential Intelligence to the broader Anuma investigation of Living Intelligence Systems.

11 — Information-Theoretic Investigation

One possible empirical route is to test whether relational and memory variables contain information about future states after conditioning on the current state.

I(Xₜ₊₁ ; Rₜ, Mₜ | Xₜ) > 0

This provides a possible bridge between the existential intelligence framework and measurable information-theoretic quantities.

The result alone would not establish Existential Intelligence as a scientific construct. Operational definitions, experimental controls and appropriate statistical analysis would still be required.

12 — Experimental Programme

A possible experimental programme compares models with different levels of contextual and relational information.

Baseline

Xₜ → Prediction

Contextual model

(Xₜ, Cₜ) → Prediction

Relational model

(Xₜ, Rₜ, Mₜ, Cₜ) → Prediction

Agency-inclusive model

(Xₜ, Rₜ, Mₜ, Cₜ, Aₜ) → Prediction

Predictive performance, calibration, robustness and other predefined measures can then be compared across models.

13 — Relationship to Resonance Transformation Theory

Existential Intelligence provides a higher-level representation of the variables that may participate in system transformation.

EI = f(C, M, R, A)

RTT introduces resonance as a proposed transformation variable:

Xₜ₊₁ = Xₜ + αρₜRₜ + βCₜ + εₜ

The two frameworks therefore address complementary questions: Existential Intelligence concerns the configuration of context, memory, relationships and agency, while RTT investigates how relational resonance may participate in transformation.

14 — Toward Living Intelligence

When multiple systems interact, intelligence may become a property of the network rather than of isolated nodes alone.

Isystem = ΣIi + Irelations + Iemergence

This creates the conceptual transition from individual existential intelligence toward Living Intelligence Systems.

The research question becomes whether the structure of relationships can contribute measurable system-level properties that are not reducible to the independent properties of individual components.

15 — Scientific Positioning

Established Foundations

The framework draws on established concepts from information theory, network science, complex systems, dynamical systems and distributed cognition.

Proposed Construct

Existential Intelligence and the formulation EI = f(C,M,R,A) are proposed components of the Anuma research framework.

Open Empirical Question

Whether these variables provide measurable explanatory or predictive value must be determined through appropriately designed experiments.

This distinction is central to the scientific positioning of the programme. Existential Intelligence is presented here as a research construct and hypothesis-generating framework, not as an already established scientific measurement.

16 — Anuma Research Core

Existential Intelligence forms one of the principal research domains of Anuma, connecting the study of intelligence with context, continuity, relationship, agency and transformation.

Context → Relationship → Resonance → Transformation → Memory → Future Context

The objective is to develop a mathematically expressible and experimentally testable research programme for intelligence across biological, technological and social systems.
