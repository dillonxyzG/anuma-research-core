Existential Intelligence

A proposed framework for understanding intelligence through context, memory, relationship and agency.

Author: Sean Adegunloye Esan ANUMA Independent Research Core Research ID: ANUMA-EI-003 Document Status: Proposed Framework
Abstract

Existential Intelligence is proposed within the ANUMA research programme as a framework for examining intelligence in relation to the conditions through which an entity exists, remembers, relates and acts.

The framework represents existential intelligence as a proposed function of context, memory, relationship and agency:

EI = f(C, M, R, A)

Rather than treating intelligence exclusively as computation or task performance, the framework asks how an evolving system uses its contextual position, historical continuity, relationships and available agency in determining subsequent states and actions.

This document develops the proposed mathematical abstraction and identifies pathways through which it could become experimentally investigated.

1. The Central Question

What does a system know about its existence, its context, its relationships and its possible actions?

The framework begins with a distinction between intelligence as isolated task performance and intelligence as an evolving property of a system situated within an environment.

A system may process information and produce outputs without necessarily maintaining continuity between its previous condition, present context and possible future actions.

Existential Intelligence therefore focuses on the structure connecting these dimensions.

2. Formal Representation

The proposed formulation is:

EI = f(C, M, R, A)

where:

  • C represents context;
  • M represents memory;
  • R represents relationship;
  • A represents agency.

The function f is intentionally left open because the appropriate mathematical relationship between these variables is a research question rather than an established result.

3. Context

Context represents the conditions within which a system exists and interprets information.

Context

The surrounding state, environment, circumstances and relevant conditions that influence interpretation and action.

The framework therefore does not treat information as universally meaningful independent of circumstance.

The same input may produce different consequences depending upon the state and context of the system receiving it.

4. Memory

Memory introduces temporal continuity.

Without some representation of previous states or interactions, a system may respond primarily to its present input. With memory, previous interactions can become part of the information available to future decisions.

Mt+1 = H(Mt, Xt, Rt)

This formulation represents memory as an evolving component rather than a static archive.

5. Relationship

Relationship represents the connections through which a system interacts with other entities, components or environmental structures.

Within the broader ANUMA framework, relationship is not treated merely as communication between isolated units. Relationships can influence the information available to future states.

Rt → Rt+1

Thus, relationships themselves may evolve as a consequence of previous interactions.

6. Agency

Agency represents the capacity of a system to participate in the selection or execution of possible actions.

Agency

The action space through which an evolving system can respond to its context and modify subsequent states.

Within the proposed framework, agency connects knowledge and action.

Context may describe where the system is. Memory may describe where it has been. Relationship may describe what it is connected to. Agency concerns what the system can do within those conditions.

7. Existential State

The four dimensions can be incorporated into the broader system state:

St = (Xt, Rt, Mt, Ct, At)

Existential Intelligence can therefore be interpreted as a property of the relationship between the system and these dimensions.

EI,t = f(Ct, Mt, Rt, At)

8. Existential Intelligence as a Dynamic Process

Existential Intelligence is not proposed as a fixed scalar property of an entity.

Because context, memory, relationship and agency can all change, the proposed quantity may also change through time.

EI,t → EI,t+1

The research problem therefore becomes one of studying evolving configurations rather than assigning a permanent intelligence value to a system.

9. Existential Intelligence and Transformation

The framework connects directly with Resonance Transformation Theory.

An evolving system can be represented as:

St+1 = F(Xt, Rt, Mt, Ct, At, ρt)

Existential Intelligence therefore describes the dimensions through which the system is situated, while RTT provides a proposed framework for investigating how those dimensions participate in transformation.

10. Meaning and Coordination

The framework proposes that meaning can be investigated as a property emerging from relationships between information, context, memory and action.

Information → Intelligence → Meaning → Coordination

Under this view, information becomes meaningful within a relational and contextual structure.

Coordination then represents the capacity for multiple components or agents to act in relation to that shared or interacting structure.

11. Individual Existential Intelligence

At the individual-system level, the proposed framework asks whether intelligence can be understood partly through the relationship between a system's current context, history, relationships and available actions.

A simplified representation is:

EI = f(Context, Memory, Relationship, Agency)

The expression is intentionally general. Operational definitions would need to be established for each empirical domain.

12. Collective Existential Intelligence

The same abstraction can be extended from individual systems to interacting groups.

EI,system = Φ(E1, E2, ..., Rsystem, Csystem)

This does not assume that collective intelligence is simply the sum of individual intelligence.

Relationships among components may introduce additional structure that must be examined independently.

13. Information-Theoretic Investigation

One possible empirical route is to determine whether context, memory and relational variables contain information about future system states beyond the current state.

I(Xt+1; Rt, Mt | Xt) > 0

This formulation asks whether relational structure and memory contain conditional information about future states.

It provides one possible empirical connection between Existential Intelligence and the information-theoretic investigation already proposed within the wider ANUMA programme.

14. Proposed Experimental Programme

A possible experimental programme can compare systems with different amounts of contextual, relational and historical information available to their predictive or adaptive processes.

Model A — State-Centred

Xt+1 = F(Xt)

Model B — Contextual

Xt+1 = F(Xt, Ct)

Model C — Relational and Historical

Xt+1 = F(Xt, Rt, Mt, Ct)

Their predictive or adaptive performance can then be compared under a defined experimental protocol.

The purpose would be to determine whether adding these dimensions produces measurable explanatory or predictive improvements.

15. The Existential Intelligence Loop

The proposed architecture can be represented as a continuous loop:

Context → Perception → Relationship → Memory → Interpretation → Agency → Action → New Context

Action changes the conditions under which the next cycle occurs. The system therefore participates in constructing the future context from which subsequent intelligence is generated.

16. Connection to Living Intelligence

Existential Intelligence extends naturally into the study of systems whose components continuously interact and adapt.

Isystem = ΣIi + Irelations + Iemergence

In this broader formulation, the context, memory, relationships and actions of individual components may participate in the development of system-level properties.

17. Scaling the Framework

The same conceptual structure may be investigated at different levels of organisation.

  • Individual systems
  • Human–machine systems
  • Learning environments
  • Organizations
  • Distributed networks
  • Communities
  • Large-scale social systems

At each scale, the empirical challenge is to define what constitutes context, memory, relationship and agency and then determine how these variables influence system behaviour.

18. ANUMA and Existential Intelligence

Within ANUMA, Existential Intelligence forms a conceptual bridge between mathematical intelligence research and the design of ambient, relational systems.

The Anuma Learning Station provides a physical research environment in which environmental, educational, human and relational signals can potentially be observed as parts of an evolving context.

The intended research direction is therefore:

Sense → Contextualize → Relate → Resonance → Transform → Remember

19. Scientific Positioning

Established scientific foundations

The framework can draw upon established concepts from information theory, dynamical systems, network science, complex systems, distributed cognition and machine learning.

Proposed framework

Existential Intelligence and the formulation EI = f(C,M,R,A) are proposed components of the ANUMA research programme.

Testable hypotheses

Whether context, memory, relationships and agency provide measurable predictive or adaptive value is an empirical question.

The framework therefore requires operational definitions, datasets or physical experiments, comparison models and reproducible evaluation before stronger scientific conclusions can be made.

20. Open Research Questions

  • How can existential intelligence be operationally measured?
  • How should context be represented mathematically?
  • What forms of memory contribute most strongly to future system states?
  • How can relational information be separated from ordinary correlation?
  • How should agency be represented in computational systems?
  • Can existential intelligence be measured across different system scales without assuming identical mechanisms?
  • Under what conditions does relational information produce emergent system-level effects?

21. Conclusion

Existential Intelligence proposes that the study of intelligence can be extended beyond isolated computation toward the conditions through which an evolving system exists, remembers, relates and acts.

Its central formulation is:

EI = f(C, M, R, A)

Context provides situated conditions. Memory provides continuity. Relationship provides structure between entities and events. Agency provides a pathway from information and interpretation toward action.

Together, these dimensions form a proposed research object for investigating intelligence as an evolving phenomenon.

The next scientific step is not to treat the formulation as established fact, but to operationalise its variables, construct measurable systems and determine whether the proposed relationships survive empirical testing.
