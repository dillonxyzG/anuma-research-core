Living Intelligence Systems

A proposed framework for investigating intelligence as an emergent property of interacting components, relationships, networks and evolving environments.

Author: Sean Adegunloye Esan ANUMA Independent Research Core Research ID: ANUMA-LIS-004 Document Status: Proposed Framework
Abstract

Living Intelligence Systems is a proposed research framework for investigating intelligence in systems whose components interact continuously through relationships, information exchange, adaptation and feedback.

The framework extends the transactional–relational and existential intelligence models from individual or local system descriptions toward networks and collective structures.

A central proposition is that system-level intelligence may not be completely represented by the intelligence of individual components alone. Relational structure and emergent dynamics may contribute additional properties that require independent investigation.

Isystem = ΣIi + Irelations + Iemergence

The expression is proposed as a research abstraction, not as an established universal law.

1. The Central Question

Can intelligence emerge from relationships between intelligent or adaptive components?

Traditional system descriptions may begin with individual components and then examine how they interact.

The Living Intelligence Systems framework reverses the emphasis by asking whether the relationships themselves can become an important part of the system's intelligence.

The research problem is therefore not simply to determine how intelligent each component is, but to investigate what happens when those components form a persistent, evolving network.

2. System Representation

A living or evolving intelligence system can be represented as a collection of interacting states:

St = (Xt, Rt, Mt, Ct, At)

where:

  • Xt — system state;
  • Rt — relational structure;
  • Mt — memory;
  • Ct — context;
  • At — agency.

In a living intelligence system, these variables may change continuously as a consequence of interaction.

3. Network Structure

The relational architecture can be represented as a graph:

G = (V, E, W)

where:

  • V represents nodes or components;
  • E represents relationships between nodes;
  • W represents relationship weights.

The graph is not necessarily static.

Gt → Gt+1

New relationships can appear, existing relationships can strengthen or weaken, and components can change their position within the network.

4. Evolving Relational Structure

A central feature of the framework is that relationships themselves participate in system dynamics.

Rt+1 = G(Rt, Xt, Ct)

This creates a feedback relationship between system state and network structure.

The system therefore does not simply operate on a fixed network. Its interactions may alter the network through which future interactions occur.

5. Emergent Intelligence

The framework proposes three conceptually distinct sources of system-level intelligence:

Isystem = ΣIi + Irelations + Iemergence
Component intelligence

Intelligence associated with individual nodes or components.

Relational intelligence

Information and structure represented by relationships among components.

Emergent intelligence

System-level properties arising through interaction among components and relationships.

The third term is especially important because emergence cannot be assumed merely from the existence of many components. It requires investigation of the interaction structure that produces system-level behaviour.

6. Distributed Intelligence

In a distributed intelligence system, no single component necessarily contains the complete information required to describe the behaviour of the entire system.

Information can instead be distributed across nodes, relationships and environmental structures.

Intelligence can be distributed across the system, rather than concentrated in a single node.

This provides a conceptual connection between living intelligence, distributed cognition, multi-agent systems, network science and adaptive environments.

7. Information Flow

Intelligence within a network depends partly upon how information moves through relationships.

Node → Relationship → Node → State Change

As information moves through the network, it can modify states and relationships.

Information → Interaction → Transformation → Memory → Future Interaction

This creates a temporal feedback loop rather than a simple one-way information pipeline.

8. Network Memory

Memory within a living intelligence system may exist at multiple levels.

  • Memory within individual components;
  • Memory represented by persistent relationships;
  • Memory encoded within network structure;
  • Memory represented by environmental changes.

A network may therefore retain traces of previous interactions even when those interactions are no longer occurring directly.

Msystem,t+1 = H(Msystem,t, Rt, Xt)

9. Biological Systems

Biological systems provide an important domain for studying distributed and relational intelligence.

Cells, organisms, ecological systems and biological communities involve interacting components whose behaviour is influenced by relationships and environmental conditions.

The Living Intelligence Systems framework does not assume that biological systems satisfy a particular definition of intelligence. Instead, it proposes them as possible domains in which relational and emergent processes can be studied.

10. Social and Organizational Systems

Human groups and organizations can also be represented as evolving relational networks.

Individuals, institutions, resources, information and decisions can form changing structures of interaction.

Gsocial,t → Gsocial,t+1

The research question is whether system-level coordination and adaptation can be partly explained through the evolving relational structure.

11. Resonance Across Networks

Resonance Transformation Theory provides a mechanism through which relational alignment can be investigated within evolving networks.

ρ(t) = Alignment(R(t), C(t))

A proposed transformation relationship is:

Xt+1 = Xt + αρtRt + βCt + εt

Within the living-systems framework, the resonance variable may therefore become a possible bridge between network structure and system transformation.

12. The Living Intelligence Loop

Environment → Interaction → Relationship → Resonance → Transformation → Memory → New Environment

The system continuously modifies the conditions under which future interactions occur.

This produces a feedback architecture in which intelligence is investigated as an evolving process rather than a fixed property.

13. Intelligence Across Scales

The proposed framework can be examined at multiple levels.

  • Individual components
  • Small interacting groups
  • Organizations
  • Biological networks
  • Learning environments
  • Distributed technological systems
  • Communities
  • Civilizational systems

The scale of analysis changes, but the underlying question remains whether relationships and interactions contribute measurable properties beyond isolated components.

14. Civilizational Intelligence

At larger scales, the framework proposes investigating intelligence as a property of interconnected social, technological, ecological and institutional systems.

A civilizational system may contain many distinct subsystems whose relationships influence information flow, coordination and adaptation.

Icivilization = Φ(Iindividual, Irelations, Iinstitutions, Ienvironment)

This is a proposed abstraction for research rather than a claim that civilization possesses a single measurable intelligence value.

15. Experimental Question

Can a system containing relational memory and evolving network structure predict or adapt to future states better than a comparable system that treats interactions as independent transactions?

I(Xt+1; Rt, Mt | Xt) > 0

Conditional mutual information provides one possible empirical test of whether relational and memory variables contain information about future system states beyond the current state.

16. Proposed Experimental Programme

Model A — Independent Components

Xi,t+1 = F(Xi,t)

Components are modelled primarily through their individual states.

Model B — Connected Components

Xi,t+1 = F(Xi,t, Ri,t)

Relational information is introduced.

Model C — Living Network

St+1 = F(Xt, Rt, Mt, Ct, At, ρt)

The complete evolving system includes relational structure, memory, context, agency and proposed resonance.

Comparative evaluation can then investigate predictive accuracy, adaptation, coordination, stability or other pre-defined measures appropriate to the experimental domain.

17. Emergence as a Research Problem

Emergence should not be treated as an explanation by itself.

A system-level property should be connected to measurable interactions, dynamics or structural changes wherever possible.

The framework therefore asks:

Which system properties cannot be adequately explained by examining isolated components alone?

This converts emergence from a descriptive label into a potential empirical research problem.

18. Anuma Learning Station

The Anuma Learning Station provides a physical experimental environment for investigating the framework.

Its proposed architecture follows:

Environment → Sensing → Context → Relationship → Resonance → Transformation → Memory

Environmental and interaction signals can therefore be treated as observations within an evolving system rather than as isolated measurements alone.

The Learning Station represents a potential bridge between mathematical modelling and physical experimentation.

19. Distributed Living Intelligence

A distributed implementation may consist of multiple local intelligent nodes.

Node1 ↔ Node2 ↔ Node3 ↔ ... ↔ Noden

Each node can maintain local observations while the network provides relational structure connecting those observations.

This architecture permits investigation of intelligence without requiring all computation or information to be concentrated in a single central system.

20. Scientific Positioning

Established scientific foundations

The framework connects with established research areas including network science, complex systems, information theory, distributed cognition, multi-agent systems, dynamical systems and machine learning.

Proposed framework

Living Intelligence Systems and the proposed decomposition into component, relational and emergent intelligence are elements of the ANUMA research programme.

Testable hypotheses

Whether relationships and network dynamics produce measurable predictive, adaptive or emergent advantages remains an empirical question.

21. Open Research Questions

  • How should relational intelligence be measured at the network level?
  • How can emergent intelligence be distinguished from aggregated component intelligence?
  • What network structures produce persistent adaptive behaviour?
  • How does memory become distributed across a network?
  • Can local intelligent nodes produce measurable system-level intelligence?
  • How does resonance influence evolving network states?
  • Can living intelligence be investigated consistently across biological, technological and social systems?

22. Conclusion

Living Intelligence Systems extends the ANUMA research programme from individual system dynamics toward networks, distributed intelligence and emergence.

Its central proposition is that a system may contain intelligence not only within its individual components but also within the relationships connecting those components and within the emergent dynamics generated by their interaction.

Components + Relationships + Interaction → System-Level Intelligence

The mathematical formulation remains a proposed research abstraction. Its scientific value depends on operational definitions, controlled experiments, measurable variables and reproducible results.

The resulting research direction is therefore to move from isolated computation toward the empirical investigation of evolving relational systems.
