Transactional–Relational Intelligence Principle

From transaction and execution toward continuity, context, relationship and emergent intelligence.

Author: Sean Adegunloye Esan ANUMA Independent Research Core Research ID: ANUMA-RTI-001 Document Status: Research Framework
Abstract

This research framework proposes a distinction between transactional and relational modes of intelligence. Transactional systems are primarily organized around exchange, execution and immediate output, whereas relational systems incorporate continuity, memory, context and evolving relationships into subsequent states.

The central proposition is that intelligence cannot be adequately described only by the capacity to execute isolated operations. In systems where relationships persist across time, the structure of those relationships may contribute to prediction, adaptation, coordination and transformation.

The framework therefore investigates whether relational information provides measurable predictive or adaptive value beyond transactional information alone.

1. Central Principle

“Transactions execute actions; relationships construct intelligence.”

The principle proposes a conceptual distinction between two modes of system organization.

A transaction generally represents an exchange or operation producing an immediate result. A relationship introduces continuity between states by preserving information about previous interactions, contextual conditions and dependencies.

The research question is therefore not whether transactions are necessary. Transactions remain fundamental to system operation. The question is whether intelligence develops differently when transactions occur inside persistent relational structures.

2. Transactional Intelligence

Transactional intelligence refers to intelligence expressed through discrete operations, exchanges or responses.

Transactional mode

Input → Operation → Output

A transactional system can be represented as a sequence of state transitions in which an input is processed to generate an output.

Examples may include computation, database transactions, command execution, automated responses, financial exchanges or isolated classification tasks.

Transactional intelligence can therefore be useful for measuring immediate performance, efficiency and execution. However, an isolated transaction does not necessarily represent the complete historical or relational structure surrounding the interaction.

3. Relational Intelligence

Relational intelligence introduces continuity into the description of an intelligent system.

IR = f(R, M, C)

where:

  • R represents relational structure;
  • M represents memory or retained history;
  • C represents contextual information.

Under this formulation, intelligence is not treated only as a function of an isolated input and output. The system's relationships, history and surrounding context become potential explanatory variables.

This creates a transition from:

Input → Output

toward:

Context → Relationship → Interaction → Memory → Future Context

4. Continuity and Memory

A central feature of the relational model is continuity. Interactions occurring at one point in time may influence the structure of subsequent interactions.

Instead of treating every interaction as independent, the system maintains some representation of previous states.

St = (Xt, Rt, Mt, Ct, At)

where:

  • Xt = system state;
  • Rt = relational structure;
  • Mt = memory;
  • Ct = context;
  • At = agency or available action.

The system therefore becomes a temporally evolving object rather than a collection of independent transactions.

5. Transactional–Relational Intelligence

The framework proposes that transactional and relational intelligence may be represented jointly.

I = IT + λIR

where:

  • IT represents transactional intelligence;
  • IR represents relational intelligence;
  • λ represents the contribution assigned to relational structure.

This equation is proposed as a modelling hypothesis rather than an established scientific law.

The purpose of the formulation is to make the proposed distinction experimentally investigable.

6. Nonlinear Relational Intelligence

The contribution of relationships may not necessarily be additive.

A more general representation is therefore:

I = Φ(IT, IR)

where Φ represents a potentially nonlinear interaction between transactional and relational components.

This allows future research to investigate whether relationships merely add information or whether particular relational configurations generate emergent effects that cannot be explained by summing isolated components.

7. Relational Breakdown

A system may continue to execute transactions while simultaneously losing relational continuity.

This creates a distinction between operational functionality and relational intelligence.

A system can therefore remain capable of processing transactions while becoming less capable of maintaining context, continuity, trust, coordination or adaptive relationships.

The framework identifies this condition as a possible relational breakdown and proposes that it should be studied independently from simple transactional failure.

8. Data Extraction and Intelligence Development

The framework distinguishes between extracting information from interactions and developing intelligence through relationships.

In a purely transactional architecture, interactions can be treated primarily as sources of data.

In a relational architecture, interaction may instead contribute to an evolving structure of memory, context, coordination and future behaviour.

The distinction can therefore be expressed conceptually as:

Interaction → Data

versus:

Interaction → Relationship → Memory → Context → Intelligence

9. Information-Theoretic Hypothesis

A central testable proposition is whether relational and memory variables contain information about future system states that cannot be explained by the current state alone.

I(Xt+1; Rt, Mt | Xt) > 0

If this conditional mutual information is greater than zero under an appropriate experimental design, the result would indicate that relational structure and memory contain predictive information about the future state beyond the information contained in the current state.

This does not by itself establish the complete transactional–relational theory. It provides a measurable pathway for investigating one of its central claims.

10. Proposed Experimental Programme

The framework proposes comparing systems under two broad modelling conditions.

Baseline: Transactional Model

The baseline model uses primarily immediate input, system state and output relationships.

Xt → Xt+1

Relational Model

The relational model incorporates relational structure, memory and contextual variables.

Xt, Rt, Mt, Ct → Xt+1

Their predictive performance can then be compared using an explicitly defined performance measure.

ΔP = Prelational − Ptransactional

A positive ΔP under controlled conditions would provide evidence relevant to the hypothesis that relational information contributes predictive value.

11. Relationship to Resonance Transformation Theory

The Transactional–Relational Intelligence Principle forms one component of the broader Resonance Transformation Theory research programme.

Within that programme, relational structure becomes relevant not only because relationships contain information, but because their alignment may influence transformation.

A proposed representation is:

Xt+1 = Xt + αρtRt + βCt + εt

where ρt represents a proposed resonance or alignment coefficient.

The equation is a proposed research model for investigating whether relational alignment can be associated with measurable transformation in system state.

12. Application Across Systems

The framework is intended as a general research abstraction rather than a claim that all systems possess the same form of intelligence.

Potential domains of investigation include:

  • Artificial and computational systems
  • Biological systems
  • Human learning environments
  • Social and organizational systems
  • Distributed intelligent systems
  • Ambient computing environments
  • Living intelligence research

In each case, the relevant question is whether persistent relationships and contextual history provide measurable explanatory or predictive information.

13. From Relational Intelligence to Living Intelligence

The relational framework extends naturally into networked systems in which intelligence may emerge from interactions among multiple components.

Isystem = ΣIi + Irelations + Iemergence

This formulation proposes that the intelligence of a system may contain at least three conceptually distinct components: intelligence of individual elements, intelligence represented by their relationships, and emergent properties generated by the system as a whole.

This provides a bridge between transactional–relational intelligence and the broader ANUMA investigation of living intelligence systems.

14. Scientific Positioning

This document should be interpreted according to three distinct levels of scientific status.

Established science

Existing scientific concepts and methods used to investigate information, networks, prediction, dynamical systems and intelligence.

Proposed framework

The Transactional–Relational Intelligence Principle, its mathematical representations and its proposed relationship to RTT.

Testable hypotheses

Claims concerning whether relational structure, memory and context provide measurable predictive or adaptive value.

The mathematical expressions in this document are therefore research formulations and should not be interpreted as established physical or biological laws without empirical validation.

15. Research Direction

The Transactional–Relational Intelligence Principle proposes a shift in the unit of analysis used to investigate intelligence.

Rather than examining only what a system does in an isolated transaction, the framework asks how the system's history, relationships and context influence what it becomes capable of doing next.

The central research programme therefore moves through the sequence:

Transaction → Relationship → Continuity → Context → Resonance → Transformation → Memory → Future Context

The objective is not to assume that relational intelligence exists in a particular form, but to construct mathematical and experimental methods capable of determining when and how relational structure contributes to intelligent behaviour.
