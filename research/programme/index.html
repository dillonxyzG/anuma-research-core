ANUMA · Research Programme

A research programme for intelligence beyond isolated computation

ANUMA is an independent research programme investigating intelligence as a relational, contextual and evolving phenomenon across biological, technological and social systems.

The programme connects mathematical modelling, information theory, network science, complex systems, ambient computation, educational environments and physical experimentation.

01 · Central Principle

The research begins with a distinction

ANUMA distinguishes between systems that primarily execute transactions and systems whose intelligence depends on relationships, continuity, context and memory.

Transactions execute actions;
relationships construct intelligence.

04 · Research Development

From proposition to experiment

The programme is structured so that theoretical propositions can progressively become formal models, experimental questions and measurable comparisons.

Mathematical formulation

Define system variables, relationships, memory, context, agency and resonance in formal mathematical terms.

Network representation

Represent interacting entities and their changing relationships using graph and network structures.

Information-theoretic investigation

Examine whether relational structure and memory provide information about future states beyond the current state alone.

Dynamical systems investigation

Study how system states evolve over time as relationships, context and resonance change.

Physical experimentation

Implement experimental environments such as the Anuma Learning Station to connect theoretical variables with observable system behaviour.

Distributed systems

Investigate how multiple local environments can form larger networks of interacting intelligence.

05 · Methods

A multidisciplinary experimental programme

ANUMA does not depend on a single scientific method. The research questions require complementary mathematical, computational and empirical approaches.

Mathematical Modelling

Formal representations of states, relationships, resonance, memory and transformation.

Network Science

Graph representations of interacting entities and evolving relational structures.

Information Theory

Investigation of whether relational and historical variables contain predictive information.

Dynamical Systems

Study of state transitions and temporal evolution within interacting systems.

Complex Systems

Investigation of emergence, distributed behaviour and system-level intelligence.

Physical Prototyping

Experimental implementation through ambient sensing, local computation and interactive environments.

06 · Core Experimental Question

Does relational information matter?

One central test proposed within the programme is whether relational structure and memory contain information about future system states beyond the present state itself.

I(Xt+1; Rt, Mt | Xt) > 0

This is treated as a testable information-theoretic hypothesis, not as an established result.

07 · Scientific Positioning

What is established, proposed and testable

The research programme deliberately distinguishes existing scientific knowledge from new theoretical propositions and hypotheses requiring empirical validation.

Established Science

Existing bodies of mathematics, information theory, network science, complex systems and related disciplines provide the scientific foundations used by the programme.

Proposed Framework

ANUMA develops proposed mathematical and conceptual relationships connecting state, context, memory, relationships and resonance.

Testable Hypotheses

Predictions concerning relational information, transformation and emergent intelligence are intended to be investigated experimentally.

ANUMA therefore presents its theoretical constructs as a research programme rather than treating proposed concepts as already established scientific laws.
